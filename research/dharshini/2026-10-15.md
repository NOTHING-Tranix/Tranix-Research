Research Topic: Trust, Documentation and Freight Payment Challenges in Indian Truck Transportation — LOADPROOF

## 1. What I Researched

I reviewed the LOADPROOF solution proposal, which focuses on freight-payment uncertainty in Indian truck transportation. The research examines how freight agreements, payment responsibilities, transport documents, deductions, and payment records can become scattered across phone calls, messages, paper documents, and separate digital systems.

The proposed solution, LOADPROOF, is a **Pre-Trip Freight Agreement and Payment-Dispute Prevention Assistant**. Its main purpose is to verify important freight terms before a trip begins, identify possible settlement risks, record changes during transportation, and organise payment evidence after delivery.

## 2. What I Found

### Finding 1: Multiple stakeholders are involved in freight settlement

A freight transaction may involve goods owners, transport companies, brokers, truck owners, drivers, receiving businesses, and payment-approval teams. The person who arranges the truck may not be the person who approves or releases the payment.

### Finding 2: Missing or unclear agreements can cause settlement problems

Freight rates, advance payments, balance amounts, due dates, and additional charges may be communicated through calls or messages without a single consolidated record. This can make it difficult to verify the original agreement.

### Finding 3: Different documents serve different purposes

Important documents include the lorry receipt/goods receipt (LR/GR), freight invoice, e-way bill where applicable, proof of delivery (POD), and payment references. A POD supports delivery verification but does not prove that payment has been made.

### Finding 4: Unrecorded deductions and payment mismatches create uncertainty

Additional charges, waiting-time expenses, and deductions may lead to disagreements if their reasons and approval status are not recorded. Invoice amounts and recorded payments may also differ from the original agreement and require reconciliation.

### Finding 5: LOADPROOF proposes four main components

1. **Pre-Trip Payment Protection Checklist:** Confirms freight amount, advance, balance, payment due date, payer details, required documents, and deduction conditions.
2. **AI-Assisted Dispute Early-Warning System:** Flags missing documents, invoice mismatches, unexplained deductions, overdue balances, and unconfirmed agreement changes.
3. **Payment Responsibility and Escalation Map:** Records who confirms the agreement, submits documents, approves invoices, releases payments, and receives follow-up requests.
4. **Chronological Trip Record:** Maintains trip details, agreement changes, documents, deductions, payments, outstanding balances, timestamps, and an evidence report.

### Finding 6: The system should support human verification

The initial prototype can use rule-based checks to identify missing or inconsistent information. AI may assist with extracting document details or summarising discrepancies. However, a warning should not automatically be treated as proof of fraud or legal responsibility.

### Finding 7: The solution needs testing before its impact can be established

The prototype can be tested using fictional trip scenarios involving missing documents, disputed deductions, partial payments, overdue balances, and invoice mismatches. Its accuracy can be measured through balance calculations, discrepancy detection, document checks, and report completeness.

### Finding 8: Existing platforms must be considered

The proposal recognises that existing freight platforms may already provide booking, tracking, document management, invoicing, and payment-related features. Therefore, LOADPROOF should not claim that these capabilities do not exist elsewhere.

Its proposed innovation is the integration of **pre-trip verification, payment-responsibility mapping, early warnings, and evidence-linked settlement reconciliation** within one trip-level workflow. This is a research hypothesis that still requires validation.

## 3. My Thoughts

I found that LOADPROOF focuses on preventing payment-related confusion before a truck starts its journey, rather than only recording payments after delivery.

Maintaining the original freight agreement, changes, documents, deductions, and payments in one chronological record could make settlement information easier to review.

However, the proposed benefits are not yet proven. The next step should be to test the prototype using different trip scenarios, compare its features with existing platforms, and collect feedback from truck owners, transporters, or brokers where feasible.

## 4. Questions / Doubts

1. Which existing freight platforms already provide similar pre-trip verification and payment-reconciliation features?
2. What information must be confirmed before a truck is dispatched?
3. How should additional charges and changes to the original freight agreement be recorded?
4. How can the system distinguish approved deductions from disputed deductions?
5. How can sensitive commercial information and payment references be protected?
6. Can the prototype accurately detect missing documents and calculate outstanding balances?
7. How can we evaluate whether LOADPROOF provides value beyond existing freight platforms?
8. Can real freight stakeholders participate in testing and provide feedback?
