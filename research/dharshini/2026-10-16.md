## 1. What I Researched

Today, I explored the challenges related to freight payment, transport documentation, invoice verification, and deductions faced by truck owners and drivers. I also reviewed existing digital freight platforms to understand their current capabilities and identify a possible improvement for our proposed solution, **LoadProof**.

The main focus was on a specific scenario: when goods remain in a warehouse longer than expected, storage charges may arise and could be deducted from the freight amount payable to the truck owner or driver. I explored how the responsibility for the delay, supporting evidence, agreed storage terms, and invoice calculations could be verified before accepting such a deduction.

I also considered how a digital system could maintain a chronological trip record containing the freight agreement, warehouse arrival and departure times, supporting documents, additional charges, invoice details, and payment status.

## 2. What I Found — 7 Evidence-Based Findings

### Finding 1: Transportation requires supporting documents

The Government of India's e-Way Bill system identifies documents such as invoices, delivery challans, transport document numbers, and vehicle details as important parts of goods movement documentation. The applicable requirements depend on the type of movement and relevant rules.

**Relevance:** LoadProof could link relevant transportation documents to each trip so that information can be reviewed during invoice verification.

**Source:** [GST E-Way Bill System — Official FAQs](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/ewb_qna.html)

### Finding 2: Errors in transport documentation can require corrective action

The official e-Way Bill FAQs explain that a generated e-Way Bill generally cannot be edited freely when incorrect information has been submitted; cancellation and generation of a corrected document may be necessary under the applicable rules.

**Relevance:** Recording document references and checking key details before dispatch could help identify mistakes earlier. LoadProof should support verification rather than attempt to replace official document systems.

**Source:** [GST E-Way Bill System — Official FAQs](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/ewb_qna.html)

### Finding 3: Existing platforms already provide digital delivery proof and freight-charge auditing

Freight Tiger publicly describes digital proof of delivery (ePOD), freight invoicing, and invoice auditing against agreed contracts. It also describes identifying freight-charge discrepancies before payment.

**Relevance:** Basic invoice checking and document storage alone are not sufficient to establish LoadProof's uniqueness. Our research must examine whether a focused warehouse-delay and storage-deduction workflow offers additional value.

**Source:** [Freight Tiger — Transportation Management System](https://www.freighttiger.com/transportation-management-system/)

### Finding 4: Warehouse storage charges need to be examined in context

When goods remain in a warehouse longer than planned, an additional storage charge may be claimed. However, the amount alone does not establish who is responsible for the delay or whether the charge should be deducted from the truck owner's freight.

This is a scenario identified for investigation, not a verified finding about how frequently such deductions occur across India.

**Relevance:** LoadProof could record the applicable storage terms, the delay timeline, the reason provided, and the supporting evidence before marking a deduction as approved.

### Finding 5: A trip timeline could help explain delays

A chronological record of arrival, unloading, warehouse release, and departure times could help the parties examine when a delay occurred. Supporting records may include warehouse receipts, timestamps, delivery documents, and approved communications.

**Relevance:** LoadProof could link these records to the trip and storage-charge entry. Timestamps alone would not prove who caused the delay; the evidence and agreed terms would still need review.

### Finding 6: Freight deductions should be traceable to the agreement and supporting evidence

Suppose the agreed freight is ₹50,000 and a storage deduction of ₹5,000 is claimed. The remaining amount would be ₹45,000 only if that deduction is valid and accepted. If it is disputed, the system should display the claimed deduction separately instead of treating it as automatically approved.

**Relevance:** LoadProof could compare the original freight agreement, storage-charge terms, supporting records, invoice, and payment entries. It could flag missing evidence, unapproved charges, or calculation mismatches for review.

This is a proposed workflow that requires validation with transport stakeholders.

### Finding 7: LoadProof needs a more focused role than a general freight-management platform

Existing platforms already offer several functions related to freight procurement, shipment visibility, delivery confirmation, invoicing, and auditing. Therefore, LoadProof should not be presented as the first system to provide these general features.

**Relevance:** A possible differentiator to investigate is the integration of warehouse-delay evidence, storage-charge verification, responsibility review, and freight-deduction reconciliation within a single trip record, particularly for smaller truck owners and operators who may not use a full transportation-management system.

This potential gap has not yet been confirmed through a complete market comparison or direct stakeholder interviews.

## 3. My Thoughts

Based on today's research, I propose refining LoadProof with a feature called **Warehouse Storage Charge Verification and Freight Deduction Protection**.

The proposed workflow is:

1. Record the truck's arrival, unloading, and warehouse-release times.
2. Record the agreed free-storage period and applicable additional-charge terms, where available.
3. Attach supporting documents and record the stated reason for the delay.
4. Compare the claimed storage charge with the agreed terms and available evidence.
5. Flag unclear responsibility, missing proof, or calculation mismatches for review.
6. Link the reviewed charge to the final freight invoice and payment record.

For example, if ₹5,000 is deducted from an agreed freight amount of ₹50,000, LoadProof would show the deduction, its stated reason, supporting evidence, and review status. It would not automatically decide that the driver or warehouse is responsible.

This approach could improve traceability and make the settlement process easier to review. However, its practical usefulness and originality must be evaluated against existing platforms and actual transport practices.
