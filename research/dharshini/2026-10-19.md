**Research Topic:** LoadProof — MVP Planning and Freight Deduction Verification

## 1. What I Researched

Today, I explored how to approach the LoadProof idea as a simple working application. I focused on defining the Minimum Viable Product (MVP), planning the dashboard, identifying essential features, and understanding how the system could help with freight payment transparency and warehouse storage deductions.

I also considered how LoadProof could be developed step by step without adding unnecessary features in the initial stage.

## 2. What I Found

**Finding 1: Start with an MVP**

Instead of building a complete application immediately, we can first develop an MVP with only the essential features. This will help us test whether the proposed solution is useful before expanding it.

**Finding 2: Dashboard and User Interface**

The application could have a simple dashboard with sections such as Dashboard, My Trips, Documents, Payments, Trip Timeline, and Dispute Alerts. Truck owners and transport office users could access relevant trip information in one place.

**Finding 3: Trip-Wise Records**

Each trip could have its own record containing the truck details, customer information, agreed freight amount, advance payment, remaining balance, payment due date, and additional charges.

**Finding 4: Document Upload and Verification Alerts**

Users could upload freight agreements, warehouse receipts, delivery proof, and other supporting documents. If a storage-charge deduction is claimed without sufficient evidence, the system could flag it as “Needs Review” instead of automatically approving it.

**Finding 5: Payment Tracking**

The system could calculate the remaining freight balance after recording the agreed freight and advance payment. Claimed deductions could be displayed separately so users can identify the amount under review.

For example, if the agreed freight is ₹50,000 and the advance received is ₹10,000, the balance before deductions is ₹40,000. If a ₹5,000 storage deduction is claimed without sufficient proof, it should remain under review.

**Finding 6: Initial Technology and Development Plan**

The initial application could use HTML, CSS, Bootstrap, and JavaScript for the frontend, Node.js and Express for the backend, and MongoDB for storing trip and payment records.

The development can begin with the dashboard and trip form, followed by document uploads, payment tracking, and rule-based verification alerts.

**Finding 7: Existing Platforms and the Need for Validation**

Platforms such as Freight Tiger and Vahak already provide various digital freight-management features. Therefore, LoadProof should not claim that digital records, tracking, or payment-related features are entirely new.

Further comparison is needed to determine whether the specific workflow connecting warehouse delays, storage-charge evidence, agreed terms, and final freight deductions offers additional value.

## 3. My Thoughts

I think we should begin with a simple MVP instead of developing too many features at once. The main goal of LoadProof should be to improve transparency by connecting trip details, documents, and payment records.

The system should flag missing evidence and discrepancies for human review rather than automatically deciding who is responsible for a delay or approving a deduction.

After developing the basic version, we should test it with truck owners and transport office users to understand whether it solves a real problem. We should also compare existing platforms before finalising the unique features of LoadProof.
