Research Topic: Trust, Documentation and Freight Payment Challenges in Indian Truck Transportation

What I Researched
  Reviewed how freight transactions involve goods owners, transport companies, brokers, independent truck owners, drivers and consignees.
  Compared direct bookings with broker-mediated and transport-company-mediated arrangements.
  Studied the difference between payment terms, such as advance payment or payment after POD acceptance, and payment methods, such as UPI, NEFT, IMPS, RTGS, cash and cheques.
  Examined how freight agreements, LR/GR, invoices, e-way bills where applicable, proof of delivery and payment references support settlement.
  Considered how missing documents, unclear deadlines, unapproved deductions and changes to freight charges can create disagreements.
  Reviewed the distinction between trucking-specific payment evidence and broader Indian MSME delayed-payment evidence.
 Examined the preliminary platform comparison to understand what existing freight applications already offer and which features still need verification.
 
What I Found
 
Finding 1: Freight payments do not follow one fixed chain
A goods owner may book directly with a truck owner or use a broker or transport company. Consequently, the truck owner may receive payment from an intermediary rather                            than from the original goods owner. This means the research must identify who agreed to pay the truck owner, rather than assuming that the shipper is always the direct payer.

Finding 2: Payment terms and payment methods are different
Payment terms determine when an amount becomes payable and what conditions must be satisfied. Payment methods determine how the money is transferred.
For example, “balance payable within seven days after POD acceptance” is a payment term, while “payment through NEFT” is a payment method. A digital transfer record alone does not establish whether the full freight amount was settled correctly.

Finding 3: Different documents establish different facts
The draft review distinguishes several types of evidence:
A freight agreement records what the parties agreed.
LR/GR and dispatch records support evidence of goods movement.
POD supports evidence of delivery.
An invoice or freight bill records the amount claimed.
A bank or payment reference supports evidence of a transfer.
The important issue is whether these records can be linked to the same trip and reconciled against the agreed freight amount.

Finding 4: Delayed payments are also a working-capital problem
The review considers how an unpaid freight balance can affect fuel purchases, driver payments, maintenance and the ability to accept another trip.
However, broader MSME delayed-payment findings must not be presented as statistics about truck owners specifically. Current trucking-specific payment-delay evidence still needs stronger verification.

Finding 5: Existing platforms must be studied before claiming a gap
The preliminary review identifies documentation, POD, invoicing and payment-related capabilities in existing freight applications.
Therefore, the project should not begin with the claim that existing platforms lack payment tracking. Further verification is needed to understand whether they already connect confirmed freight terms, amendments, documents, payments and disputed deductions within one workflow.

Finding 6: The payment-value examples are hypothetical
The amounts ₹25,000, ₹75,000 and ₹2,00,000 were used only as illustrative scenarios. They are not established national averages or recognised industry categories.
These scenarios help test advance calculations, balance payments, adjustments and outstanding amounts without making unsupported claims about actual freight rates.

Finding 7: LoadProof is a proposed evidence and reconciliation assistant
The proposed LoadProof concept would organise:
Confirmed freight terms and payment deadlines.
Timestamped changes to rates and charges.
Trip-linked documents and POD.
Advance and subsequent payment references.
Approved and disputed deductions.
Outstanding balances.
A chronological evidence pack for payment follow-up.
It is not intended to transfer money, guarantee payment or resolve disputes automatically.

 
My Thoughts
I think the project should focus on making freight-settlement records clearer and easier to reconcile, rather than claiming to solve delayed payments entirely.
A useful research question is whether one trip-linked record can reduce confusion about the agreed amount, approved adjustments, payments received and remaining balance.
The main challenge may be obtaining confirmation from the relevant parties. A record entered by only one person may not represent a mutually accepted agreement.
A simple prototype should be considered before introducing complex technology such as blockchain.
The proposed research gap remains provisional until existing platform workflows and stakeholder needs are investigated more thoroughly.

Questions / Doubts
Who should confirm freight terms when a broker or transport company arranges the trip?
What event starts the payment deadline: delivery, POD submission, POD acceptance or invoice approval?
How should the system distinguish a payment reference entered by a user from a payment actually confirmed as received?
How should an unapproved deduction affect the displayed outstanding balance?
How should revised freight rates and waiting charges be confirmed and recorded?
Do existing platforms already generate a chronological settlement-evidence pack?
What information would truck owners, brokers and customers be willing to share?
Can the prototype remain useful when the other party does not participate?
