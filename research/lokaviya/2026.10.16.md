**Research Topic:** Combined Research on Four Software Problem Areas in Indian Truck Logistics

## What I Researched

I reviewed four connected problem areas that a truck-logistics software system could help address:

1. Trip profitability and operating costs.
2. Empty return trips and cargo matching.
3. Vegetable spoilage risk during transportation.
4. Delivery delays, waiting time, and payment tracking.

The aim of this research is to understand the problems together before collecting detailed truck and mileage data or planning the software implementation.

## What I Found

### 1. Trip Profitability

A truck's freight revenue is not the same as its profit. Operating costs can include fuel, tolls, driver allowance, loading and unloading, maintenance, tyres, and other trip-related expenses. A useful system should compare estimated expenses with actual expenses and show estimated profit, profit per kilometre, and the difference between the estimate and actual result.

### 2. Empty Return Trips and Cargo Matching

A truck may return without cargo when the transport office cannot find a suitable load. A load-matching module could compare truck location, destination, available time, carrying capacity, cargo type, and delivery deadline. It should also estimate whether the freight offered is worth the extra distance and cost needed to collect the load.

### 3. Vegetable Spoilage Risk

Vegetable damage and spoilage can be affected by vegetable type, temperature, humidity, rain exposure, packaging, ventilation, loading method, waiting time, and journey duration. Different vegetables have different handling needs. The software could show a transparent low/medium/high risk estimate and handling suggestions, but it should not claim that spoilage has occurred unless supported by reliable inspection or sensor data.

### 4. Delays, Waiting Time, and Payments

Unplanned loading or unloading waits and delivery delays can affect truck utilisation and operating costs. Late customer payments can also affect cash flow. A tracking module could record planned and actual pickup/delivery times, waiting duration, invoice details, due dates, amounts received, and pending balances.

## How the Four Areas Connect

The modules can share one trip record. The same trip ID can connect the truck, route, cargo, revenue, expenses, planned and actual times, vegetable-risk details, and invoice status. This reduces duplicate data entry and makes the reports easier to compare.

## My Thoughts

The four problems should be treated as parts of one logistics workflow rather than four unrelated tools. First, the project should define what data each module needs and where that data will come from. The next research step is to collect comparable truck data—including new and older vehicles, fuel type, mileage, and CNG availability—because the profitability calculation depends on the vehicle and fuel assumptions.

## Questions / Doubts

- Which truck models have reliable published mileage or fuel-efficiency figures?
- Are published mileage figures comparable with real-world mileage under load?
- Which vegetable types and seasons should be prioritised for spoilage-risk rules?
- How will the system validate trip expenses, delivery times, and payment records?
- Which users should be able to create or edit each record?
