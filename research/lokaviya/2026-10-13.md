**Research Topic:** Factors Affecting Actual Trip Profitability of Truck Owners in India

## What I Researched

- Researched how truck owners in India calculate trip revenue and determine whether a transport trip is profitable.
- Studied the major expenses involved in truck operations, including fuel, tolls, driver wages, maintenance, tyres, insurance, permits, depreciation and financing costs.
- Examined how empty return journeys, waiting time at loading and unloading points, and cancelled bookings affect truck owners' earnings.
- Investigated how truck type, route distance, cargo requirements, vehicle utilisation and return-load availability influence profitability.
- Explored how software could help truck owners record expenses, calculate actual trip profit and compare estimated costs with actual costs.

## What I Found

- **Finding 1: Freight revenue does not equal profit.** A truck owner may receive a high freight payment but retain only a small amount after paying operating expenses and allocating vehicle ownership costs. The TCI–IIM Calcutta study on freight transportation efficiency highlights the difference between contribution margin and profit after overheads.

- **Finding 2: Empty return journeys reduce profitability.** When a truck delivers goods and returns without another paying load, the owner incurs additional fuel, toll, driver and vehicle costs without earning revenue for the return journey. NITI Aayog identifies empty running and vehicle utilisation as important areas for improving trucking efficiency.

- **Finding 3: Waiting time creates direct and indirect losses.** Delays at factories, warehouses and delivery locations can increase driver expenses, idling fuel and parking costs. Waiting can also prevent the truck from accepting its next booking, reducing potential earnings.

- **Finding 4: Cancellations can leave truck owners with unrecovered expenses.** If a customer cancels after the truck has travelled to the pickup location, the owner may already have spent money on fuel, driver time and vehicle positioning. Recovery of these costs depends on the booking agreement and any applicable cancellation terms.

- **Finding 5: Truck type and cargo affect the cost structure.** Different trucks have different capacities, fuel efficiencies, maintenance requirements and ownership costs. Specialised cargo may also require refrigeration, tanker cleaning, additional equipment or safety procedures. The DPIIT logistics-cost assessment discusses how cargo and handling requirements affect transportation costs.

- **Finding 6: Profitability and cash flow are different.** A truck may earn an accounting profit but still face cash-flow difficulties because customers pay late while fuel bills, driver wages and loan instalments are due. Loan principal repayment is a cash outflow, whereas interest and depreciation must be treated differently when calculating economic profit.

- **Finding 7: Software could improve trip-level financial visibility.** A transport management platform could combine freight revenue, fuel bills, tolls, driver expenses, maintenance, waiting hours, empty kilometres and ownership costs. It could calculate actual profit or loss per trip and identify routes or bookings that consistently perform poorly.

## My Thoughts

- I think truck owners need to understand the actual profit from a complete trip, including the return journey, rather than judging profitability only by the freight amount received.
- A possible opportunity could be a truck profitability dashboard that calculates total trip expenses, net earnings, profit per kilometre and the financial impact of empty running or waiting.
- The software could compare estimated expenses before a trip with actual expenses after delivery, helping owners understand why a trip performed better or worse than expected.
- One thing we should consider is that small truck owners may not record every expense digitally. The system should be simple to use and allow manual expense entry, receipt uploads and basic trip tracking.
- The platform should calculate different cost structures for different truck types, routes and cargo categories instead of assuming that all trucks have the same operating costs.
- We should validate the findings through interviews with truck owners and transport operators before claiming that a particular cost or problem affects every operator.

## Questions / Doubts

- How do small truck owners currently record and calculate the complete cost of each trip?
- How much does an empty return journey cost compared with a return journey carrying paid cargo?
- How many hours do trucks typically wait for loading, unloading or their next booking on the routes we study?
- How frequently are confirmed bookings cancelled, and what proportion of cancellation-related expenses can owners recover?
- How should monthly insurance, permits, depreciation and financing interest be allocated to individual trips?
- Would truck owners use a software tool that calculates trip profitability, and what information would they be willing and able to enter?
- How do profitability patterns differ between vegetable trucks, oil tankers and water tankers?
