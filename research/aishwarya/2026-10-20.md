# Daily Research Report

**Project:** Fleet Management and Delivery Problems  
**Date:** 20 October 2026  
**Research Area:** Stakeholder Adoption, Broker Integration, and Low-Cost Platform Development

## 1. Topic Studied

Understanding barriers to digital platform adoption among transportation stakeholders and exploring the role of brokers in connecting traditional transport operations with digital systems.

## 2. Objective

To understand how a fleet management platform could serve truck drivers, truck owners, brokers, and transport offices, including stakeholders who do not use smartphones, while keeping initial development and operating costs low.

## 3. Work Carried Out

### 3.1 Barriers to Digital Adoption

Studied potential reasons why transportation stakeholders may hesitate to use digital platforms, including:

- Limited digital literacy and language barriers.
- Lack of trust in online transactions and unfamiliar users.
- Preference for traditional communication methods, such as phone calls and established broker relationships.
- Concerns about platform fees, commissions, and unclear benefits.
- Internet connectivity limitations and complicated registration processes.

### 3.2 Role of Brokers in Digital Transportation

Explored the possibility of integrating brokers into the proposed platform instead of excluding them from the transportation network.

Potential features discussed included:

- Broker-assisted booking for drivers and owners who do not use smartphones.
- Broker profiles and identity verification.
- Transparent records of freight charges, commissions, and payment status.
- Phone-based communication and alternative notification methods.
- Matching available trucks with suitable cargo requirements.

These are proposed ideas that require validation through stakeholder interviews.

### 3.3 Low-Cost Software Development and Operations

Studied ways to minimise initial expenses when developing and testing a fleet management platform.

The following approaches were considered:

- Building a basic prototype before developing a complete system.
- Using free development tools and free-tier hosting services.
- Using existing devices and communication channels.
- Handling initial maintenance and customer support internally.
- Investigating subscriptions and transaction fees as potential revenue models.

A prototype may be developed at little or no direct cost, but production deployment can involve hosting, messaging, maintenance, security, and support expenses.

### 3.4 Understanding Transport Requirements

Studied the information required to arrange the transportation of goods, including:

- Cargo type, quantity, and weight.
- Pickup and delivery locations.
- Suitable vehicle or tanker type.
- Pickup and delivery schedules.
- Freight charges and special cargo-handling requirements.

For example, transporting milk between Chennai and Bengaluru requires suitable tanker specifications, cargo quantity, pickup and delivery details, scheduling, and agreed freight charges.

## 4. Key Learnings

1. Digital adoption depends on more than the availability of an application; trust, usability, cost, and perceived benefits also matter.
2. Brokers could potentially act as intermediaries for stakeholders who cannot or do not wish to use digital platforms directly.
3. Broker integration should be evaluated carefully because their willingness to participate may depend on how the platform affects their existing business relationships.
4. A small pilot can help validate whether the platform solves a genuine transportation problem before further investment.
5. Standardised transport requirement records could improve communication between cargo providers, brokers, truck owners, and drivers.

## 5. Research Limitations

The points discussed are preliminary observations and proposed approaches. They have not yet been validated through primary interviews or a systematic review of supporting research papers. Their actual relevance and importance to Indian transportation stakeholders remain to be established.

## 6. Next Steps

- Prepare interview questions for truck drivers, truck owners, brokers, and transport offices.
- Investigate how brokers currently arrange loads and vehicles for stakeholders without smartphones.
- Identify frequent problems in booking, communication, payments, and trust.
- Review existing transportation platforms and relevant research literature.
- Estimate the minimum requirements and operating costs for a small pilot.

## 7. Conclusion

The study focused on making a future fleet management platform accessible, practical, and affordable. Broker-assisted access emerged as a potential approach for connecting traditional transportation stakeholders with digital services. Further field research is required to identify the most significant problems and determine which proposed features would provide genuine value.
