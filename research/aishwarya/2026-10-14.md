# Daily Research Report

**Project:** Fleet Management and Delivery Problems  
**Date:** 14 October 2026

## 1. Objective

To study existing fleet management systems, identify major transportation problems, review related research papers, and explore possible research gaps for developing an improved software solution.

## 2. Research Papers Studied

**1. Routing Tankers for Dairy Milk Pickup (1994)**

- Studied milk tanker routing at a dairy plant in Uttar Pradesh.
- Used a computer-based decision-support system to improve tanker routes.
- Demonstrated how route planning could reduce transportation costs.

**2. An Operational Scheduling Framework for Tanker-Based Water Distribution Systems under Uncertainty (2023)**

- Studied water tanker scheduling when demand and travel times are uncertain.
- Used an optimisation model to improve scheduling decisions.

**3. Modeling Enablers of Transporter's Performance in Downstream Logistics of the Indian Oil Sector (2021)**

- Examined factors affecting transportation performance in India's oil sector.
- Helped identify operational factors that influence transporter performance.

**4. Cold-Food Routing Literature Review**

- Reviewed 65 research papers on routing and transportation of temperature-sensitive food.
- Highlighted the importance of delivery planning and maintaining appropriate conditions during transportation.

## 3. Existing Fleet Management Systems Studied

The following systems were examined to understand the capabilities already available in the market:

- **Fleetx:** Vehicle tracking, route optimisation, trip management and fleet analytics.
- **LocoNav:** GPS tracking, fuel monitoring, driver monitoring and fleet management.
- **MapmyIndia Cold Chain Tracking:** Vehicle tracking, temperature monitoring and alerts.
- **Watsoo Water Transport Management:** Water tanker tracking, scheduling and delivery management.

The review showed that many conventional fleet management functions are already available. Therefore, simply developing another tracking or route-planning application may not provide a sufficiently distinctive research contribution.

## 4. Transportation Problems Identified

The following problems were discussed during the research:

- High transportation costs and inefficient vehicle utilisation.
- Empty vehicle journeys and avoidable operating expenses.
- Uncertainty in travel times and delivery demand.
- Difficulties in scheduling tankers and coordinating deliveries.
- Cargo-specific concerns involving liquid cargo, perishable goods and general freight.
- Incomplete transportation records and difficulties investigating delivery discrepancies.
- Limited historical data that may make it difficult for smaller transport operators to identify recurring problems.
- Difficulty establishing what happened during a trip when records from different parties are missing or contradictory.

Common problems such as traffic congestion, route selection, loading and unloading delays, empty returns, and cold storage were also considered. These were not selected as the final research topic because the aim is to investigate a less-explored problem.

## 5. New Research Directions Explored

Three possible directions were examined:

**A. Cargo integrity and chain of custody**

Connecting loading, transportation, custody handovers and unloading records to help investigate cargo quantity or quality discrepancies.

**B. Reconstructing transportation events**

Combining timestamps, location records, photographs, measurements and delivery acknowledgements to help determine what happened during a trip.

**C. Transportation decision-making with limited or unreliable data**

Investigating how software can identify missing or contradictory information and avoid making unsupported recommendations.

Another direction considered was helping smaller transport companies identify the causes of recurring financial losses using limited operational records.

These are preliminary ideas. Their originality and research value have not yet been established.

## 6. Key Findings

1. Existing software already supports many common fleet management functions.
2. Research has addressed several transportation problems, including tanker routing and uncertain delivery scheduling.
3. Simply combining existing features would not automatically create a novel research contribution.
4. Cargo-specific requirements and incomplete transportation records may provide opportunities for further investigation.
5. A proposed solution must be compared against existing research and software before claiming that it addresses an unsolved problem.

## 7. Conclusion

The research provided an initial understanding of existing fleet management systems, transportation problems and previously proposed solutions. However, a specific research gap has not yet been confirmed.

The next stage is to investigate less-explored transportation problems, prioritising liquid cargo in India, and verify whether existing research or commercial software already addresses them adequately.
