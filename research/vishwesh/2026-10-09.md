Research Topic: Oil Lorry Transportation & Client Acquisition Workflow in India (how transport offices connect mills and lorry drivers today)

---

## What I Researched

* Researched the current solutions available for the problem: the existing transport-office process in India, where Mill A (consignor) sends oil stock to Mill B (consignee) through a transport office and a lorry owner/driver.
* Looked at how existing products solve this problem: today the "product" is the transport office itself, which works through phone calls, driver networks and personal relationships rather than software. Government tools like the e-Way Bill system already digitize one part (documents).
* Checked what technologies or approaches are currently being used: phone calls, WhatsApp/photos for LR copies, couriers, paper documents, and the web/mobile/SMS/API-based e-Way Bill system. Also checked NITI Aayog findings on market structure and technology gaps.

## What I Found

* Finding 1: **The process is intermediary-driven and informal.** Mill A does not look for a lorry itself. It tells a transport office, which phones drivers in its network to find an available vehicle. Money flows Mill A → Transport Office → Lorry Owner/Driver.
* Finding 2: **The workflow has 11 steps.** Requirement → driver search → freight rate agreed (usually per ton, e.g. ₹2,300 or ₹2,500) → documentation (LR, E-Way Bill, invoice, GST copies, insurance papers) → trip amount settled → lorry goes to Mill A → loading → transport to Mill B → delivery and unloading → LR copy confirmation → payment settlement.
* Finding 3: **The market is fragmented and under-digitized.** A NITI Aayog study reports that more than 75% of freight-market operators own fewer than five commercial goods carriers. NITI Aayog also identifies limited use of technology in load and route planning as a factor that reduces truck utilization.
* Finding 4: **Digital documents already exist.** The e-Way Bill system supports web, mobile, SMS and API generation. For applicable GST movements above ₹50,000, an e-Way Bill is required before goods move, subject to rules and exemptions.
* Finding 5: **Loading is slow and costly for the driver.** The journey doesn't really start when the lorry reaches Mill A. Waiting for labour, labour charges, material preparation and mill-side procedures can take a long time.
* Finding 6: **Getting the signed LR copy back is informal and delays payment.** The confirmed LR copy (signed/sealed by Mill B) must reach the transport office. This happens by the driver carrying it, by courier, or digitally (phone photo, WhatsApp, document sharing).
* Finding 7: **The office takes a cut, and the driver's profit is much smaller than the trip amount.** Illustrative figures from the described workflow: commission ~5% to 10%, GST/document handling ~₹200 to ₹300, LR charge ~₹100, plus other charges. Driver Profit = Trip Revenue − Commission/Charges − Fuel − Toll − Food − Other Operating Expenses. (The ₹70,000 trip amount in the source document is hypothetical, and these are not fixed industry rates.)

## My Thoughts

* I think the opportunity is not just "truck booking". The real gap is digitizing matching, coordination, documents and payment around a fragmented owner-driver market.
* I think the transport office works because of trust and relationships, so a platform probably has to either work with offices or offer something clearly better than their network, not simply bypass them.
* A possible opportunity could be digital LR copy confirmation (photo/e-signature from Mill B) so payment is not held up waiting for courier or WhatsApp forwarding.
* A possible opportunity could be transparent rate discovery and an upfront breakdown of commission and charges, replacing manual negotiation and unclear deductions.
* A possible opportunity could be tackling loading wait time (e.g. slot scheduling at mills) since it eats into driver time and earnings.
* One thing we should consider is integrating with the existing e-Way Bill system (it has an API) instead of building document generation from scratch.
* One thing we should consider is that the commission and charge figures are illustrative, so we need real numbers before designing any pricing model.

## Questions / Doubts

* Is the ~5% to 10% commission typical across offices and routes, or does it vary a lot by commodity and relationship?
* How can we win the trust of drivers and offices who currently rely on phone calls and personal contacts?
* Who is the right first client to acquire: mills (consignors), transport offices, or drivers?
* Do users actually need a full replacement for the transport office, or just tools that make its work faster (matching, documents, payments)?
* How can we handle the physical LR copy requirement, and is a digital-only confirmation accepted by mills and offices for payment?
* How can we reduce loading wait time when it depends on mill-side labour and procedures that we don't control?
