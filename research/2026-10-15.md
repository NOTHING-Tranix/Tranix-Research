# Daily Report

**Date:** 19 October 2026

**Topic:** Fleet Management and Delivery Problems – Proposed Transportation Solutions

Solution 1: Cargo Condition, Handover and Loss Tracking 
1. Problem 
• Perishable goods such as milk, fruits, and vegetables may lose quality during transportation. 
• When cargo arrives damaged or spoiled, it can be difficult to identify where the loss occurred and who was responsible. 
• Existing records may not provide enough information about cargo conditions throughout the journey. 

2. Problem Cause 
• Temperature and humidity may not be monitored continuously. 
• Cargo quality and quantity may not be checked at every handover. 
• Transporters may lack a common record of cargo condition from pickup to delivery. 
• Quality checks at delivery may identify damage without revealing when it occurred. 

3. Solution 
Develop a Cargo Condition, Handover and Loss Tracking System that monitors cargo conditions, records each transfer of responsibility, and estimates where quality deterioration may have occurred. 

4. How to Implement the Solution 
• Condition Monitoring: Use temperature and humidity sensors for selected cargo types, along with GPS to track vehicle location. 
• Digital Handover Records: Use QR codes to record cargo quantity, condition, time, and the person handing over or receiving the cargo. 
• Loss Analysis: Compare sensor readings and handover records with quality checks performed at delivery. 
• Risk Alerts: Notify operators when recorded conditions indicate a potential risk to cargo quality. 
• System Testing: Compare the system's estimated loss location with actual inspection results. 

5. Effect of Our Solution 
• Improves visibility of cargo conditions throughout transportation. 
• Helps identify the transportation stage where quality deterioration may have occurred. 
• Improves accountability and helps resolve disputes between transporters and customers. 
• May help reduce cargo losses, although this must be demonstrated through testing. 

Solution 2: Breakdown Recovery and Predictive Maintenance 
1. Problem 
• Unexpected vehicle breakdowns can interrupt transportation, delay deliveries, and increase operating costs. 
• When a vehicle carrying perishable goods breaks down, additional delays may affect cargo quality. 
• Dispatchers may struggle to decide whether to repair the vehicle, transfer the cargo, or arrange another vehicle. 

2. Problem Cause 
• Mechanical failures may occur unexpectedly, and repair times are uncertain. 
• Maintenance records may not be sufficient to anticipate potential failures. 
• Nearby replacement vehicles may not have suitable capacity or equipment. 
• Transferring cargo can introduce additional costs, delays, and handling risks. 

3. Solution 
Develop a Breakdown Recovery Decision-Support System that compares available recovery options using repair time, cost, cargo condition, and replacement-vehicle compatibility. Predictive maintenance can support the system by identifying potential maintenance needs. 

4. How to Implement the Solution 
• Breakdown Reporting: Record the vehicle's location, reported fault, cargo type, and breakdown time. 
• Repair-Time Estimation: Use available maintenance records and repair history to estimate recovery time. 
• Recovery Option Comparison: Compare repairing, towing, transferring cargo, or diverting it to a suitable nearby facility. 
• Replacement Vehicle Matching: Identify available vehicles and check their capacity, equipment, destination, and cargo compatibility. 
• Predictive Maintenance: Analyse mileage, service history, and available diagnostic data to flag possible maintenance requirements. 
• Decision Evaluation: Compare recommended actions with alternative recovery strategies using simulated or real transportation scenarios. 

5. Effect of Our Solution 
• Helps dispatchers make better-informed recovery decisions. 
• May reduce unnecessary waiting time and recovery expenses. 
• Helps preserve cargo quality when suitable alternatives are available. 
• Can improve vehicle availability through better maintenance planning. 
• The actual benefits depend on the accuracy of recovery estimates and the availability of suitable replacement vehicles. 

Solution 3: Fleet and Route Optimization — Compatibility-Aware Return Loads 
1. Problem 
• Trucks may return empty after completing deliveries, resulting in wasted vehicle capacity and fuel. 
• Specialised vehicles, such as food-grade tankers and refrigerated trucks, may not be suitable for every available return shipment. 
• Finding a compatible shipment for the return journey can be difficult when shipment information is scattered across different transporters. 

2. Problem Cause 
• Available return-load information may not be shared between vehicle owners and shippers. 
• The next shipment may be incompatible with the vehicle's previous cargo. 
• Tankers may require cleaning before carrying a different product. 
• Vehicle capacity, shipment location, destination, and handling requirements may not match. 

3. Solution 
Develop a Compatibility-Aware Return-Load Matching System that identifies suitable return shipments by considering vehicle capacity, route, previous cargo, cleaning requirements, and transportation cost—not distance alone. 

4. How to Implement the Solution 
• Vehicle Registration: Store vehicle type, capacity, current location, and cargo-handling requirements. 
• Shipment Registration: Collect available shipment details, including cargo type, quantity, pickup location, and destination. 
• Compatibility Checking: Check whether the vehicle can safely carry the next shipment and whether cleaning or other preparation is required. 
• Return-Load Matching: Identify suitable shipments and rank them using distance, cleaning time, cleaning cost, and expected trip cost. 
• Performance Tracking: Record empty kilometres, fuel expenses, vehicle utilisation, and successful return-load matches. 
• System Testing: Compare the results with ordinary distance-based matching and determine whether compatibility-based matching provides measurable benefits. 

5. Effect of Our Solution 
• May reduce empty return journeys and unnecessary fuel consumption. 
• Improves the utilisation of specialised vehicles. 
• May reduce cleaning and operating costs when suitable loads are available. 
• Improves coordination between vehicle owners and shippers. 
• The benefits for Indian tanker fleets must be verified using actual operating data. 

Solution 4: Loading, Unloading and Turnaround Optimization 
1. Problem 
• Trucks may spend considerable time waiting at warehouses, distribution centres, and loading or unloading facilities. 
• Long waiting times reduce vehicle availability and can delay subsequent deliveries. 
• Perishable cargo may lose some of its remaining acceptable shelf life while waiting for handling. 

2. Problem Cause 
• Vehicle arrival times may not be coordinated with loading-bay availability. 
• Facilities may lack accurate information about incoming vehicles. 
• Documentation and cargo verification may delay loading or unloading. 
• Fixed schedules may not account for unexpected arrivals or the urgency of perishable cargo. 

3. Solution 
Develop a Dynamic Loading, Unloading and Turnaround Optimization System that coordinates vehicle arrivals and loading slots. For facilities handling perishable goods, the system could also prioritise cargo based on its remaining acceptable time. 

4. How to Implement the Solution 
• Turnaround Data Collection: Record arrival time, start and end of loading or unloading, departure time, and total waiting time. 
• GPS-Based Arrival Notifications: Estimate vehicle arrival times and notify facility staff so they can prepare for incoming vehicles. 
• Dock Scheduling: Register available loading bays, facility capacity, and existing appointments. 
• Dynamic Slot Allocation: Assign or adjust loading and unloading slots according to expected arrivals and available resources. 
• Perishable Cargo Prioritisation: Consider remaining acceptable transportation time alongside arrival time and facility constraints when scheduling cargo. 
• Performance Testing: Compare the system with first-come-first-served and fixed-slot scheduling using simulations or facility data. 

5. Effect of Our Solution 
• May reduce vehicle waiting time and total turnaround time. 
• Improves utilisation of loading bays and facility resources. 
• Helps transporters and warehouse staff coordinate vehicle arrivals. 
• May reduce delays affecting time-sensitive cargo. 
• The improvement must be measured using actual waiting-time and turnaround-time data.

## Conclusion

The study identified four potential solutions to major transportation and fleet management problems: cargo quality deterioration, unexpected vehicle breakdowns, empty return journeys, and loading or unloading delays. These solutions aim to improve cargo tracking, recovery decisions, vehicle utilisation, and operational efficiency. Further research and testing are required to validate their feasibility and measure their effectiveness in real-world transportation scenarios.
